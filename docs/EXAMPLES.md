# Example Reflection Outputs

This document provides realistic examples of reflection outputs for both Flow A and Flow B.

## Flow A Examples (Reflection-First)

### Example 1: High Confidence Purchase

**User Answers:**
- Q1: "Noise-canceling headphones"
- Q2: "I've been thinking about it for a while"
- Q3: "Saving the money"
- Q4: "I'm glad I bought it"
- Q5: "Buy an alternative"

**Reflection Output:**

#### Summary
You're considering buying noise-canceling headphones, something that's been on your mind for some time.

#### Trade-off
Choosing this likely means setting aside the option to save this money for now.

#### Looking Ahead
You anticipate positive feelings about this. Purchases that meet ongoing needs tend to maintain their value over time.

---

### Example 2: Impulse Purchase

**User Answers:**
- Q1: "Designer sneakers"
- Q2: "It's on sale or feels limited"
- Q3: "Another purchase"
- Q4: "I barely used it"
- Q5: "Do nothing and move on"

**Reflection Output:**

#### Summary
You're considering buying designer sneakers, mainly driven by timing and current availability.

#### Trade-off
Making this purchase creates a trade-off with other things you might buy.

#### Looking Ahead
You're noticing uncertainty about future use. Items often receive different amounts of attention than we initially imagine.

#### Soft Pause
You don't need to decide right now. Revisiting this later may give you a clearer signal.

## Key Patterns in Reflections

### High-Risk Indicators
These patterns trigger a "Soft Pause" suggestion:

- Impulse timing + low expected use
- High cost + unexplored alternatives
- Emotional reaction + anticipated regret
- Limited availability + fading interest

### Confidence Indicators
These patterns receive supportive reflection:

- Long consideration + daily use
- Clear need + stable opportunity
- Researched decision + positive anticipation

### Neutral Framing
All reflections maintain neutrality by:

- Describing rather than prescribing
- Acknowledging rather than judging
- Informing rather than directing
- Respecting user autonomy

## Tone Analysis

### What Makes These Work

**Observational Language:**
- "You're considering..." not "You want to buy..."
- "This represents..." not "This costs..."
- "You're noticing..." not "You should see..."

**Uncertainty Framing:**
- "...may differ from..." not "...will be less than..."
- "...often receive..." not "...always get..."
- "...tend to..." not "...definitely will..."

**Autonomy Preservation:**
- "The decision is still available to you" not "You should wait"
- "Time could clarify this" not "Don't buy this now"
- "You don't need to decide right now" not "Wait before buying"

### What We Avoid

**Judgment:**
- ❌ "This seems like an impulse buy"
- ✅ "You're reacting to how you feel right now"

**Prediction:**
- ❌ "You'll regret this purchase"
- ✅ "Items often receive different amounts of attention than initially imagined"

**Prescription:**
- ❌ "You should save your money instead"
- ✅ "Choosing this likely means setting aside the option to save this money for now"

**Moralization:**
- ❌ "Spending on experiences is better than things"
- ✅ "Every choice to acquire something involves exchanging it for other possibilities"
